lzaoral commented at 2025-06-09 14:10:¶

I've just checked the situation in Fedora and RHEL.

The support for the linuxefi and initrdefi commands with distinct semantics was introduced in Fedora 17 [1]. The patches were restructured in Fedora 29 and linuxefi and initrdefi became mere aliases for linux and initrd [2]. The same unification can be found in RHEL 8 and newer.

Moreover, these commands were never introduced on non-x86 architectures, e.g. aarch64, so these extra entries will be broken there (when #3440 gets merged).

I can't speak for Ubuntu or SLES but at least on Fedora-based distros, these entries are redundant.

[1] https://src.fedoraproject.org/rpms/grub2/c/58a808c00980b3a2e7d44d8d309e6e6219f100ff
[2] https://src.fedoraproject.org/rpms/grub2/c/ad4aff0c12370a6edc82478d8ca83627f6916e3d

lzaoral commented at 2025-06-16 12:54:¶

lzaoral commented at 2025-09-05 12:40:¶

We have looked at this issue again with @pcahyna and the Debian patches I've linked in https://github.com/rear/rear/issues/3423#issuecomment-2976548159 contain additional changes not present in the Fedora original with the following description:

    - Automatically call linuxefi from linux if secure boot is enabled and
      the kernel is signed, to hand over to the kernel without calling
      ExitBootServices.  Otherwise, linux will fall through to previous
      code, call ExitBootServices itself, and boot the kernel normally.

In other words, even though linuxefi and linux are different commands with distinct semantics, it should be safe to always use linux on Debian and Ubuntu.

jsmeix commented at 2025-09-09 12:52:¶

It is OK to break support for releases older than SLES15 SP2
in the upcoming ReaR 3.0 because

  • there should be no SLES15 user who still uses such an old service pack (SP)
  • even if there is a SLES15 user who still uses SLES15 SP2 he should usually have no reason to update to ReaR 3.0
  • ReaR for SLES is provided via the High Availability Extension (HA) and SUSE support for SLES HA 15 SP2 ended in general on 31 Dec 2021 and Long Term Service Pack Support ended on 31 Dec 2024 see https://www.suse.com/de-de/lifecycle/#suse-linux-enterprise-high-availability-extension-15
